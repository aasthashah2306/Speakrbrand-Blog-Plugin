# Why Curiosity — Not Expertise — Is the Real AI Leadership Skill

Every time something new shows up, the first reaction is fear.

"This is going to replace us."

I'm hearing that again with AI. I've been hearing it for thirty years. And I've been building AI technology for most of that time — 95 patents worth. So let me tell you what I've actually seen happen.

**The pattern is always the same.** New technology arrives. People panic. Then the curious ones pull ahead — not because they knew more at the start, but because they were willing to learn faster than everyone else.

Here's the thing about curiosity. Most people treat it like a personality trait. You either have it or you don't.

That's wrong.

**Curiosity is a skill.** And right now, it's the most valuable leadership skill you can build.

## The Shift Nobody's Talking About

AI is reshaping leadership roles faster than most executives realize. But the conversation keeps getting stuck on the wrong question.

Everyone asks: "What can AI do?"

The better question is: **"What does AI free me to do?"**

When I was building the first AI-powered virtual assistant in the 1990s — years before Siri or Alexa existed — we weren't trying to replace people. We were trying to remove the tedious parts of work so humans could focus on the parts that actually required judgment.

That's still what's happening. It just matters more now.

-> Before AI, leaders spent 60% of time on execution
-> Now, AI handles much of that execution
-> The job shifts to judgment, strategy, and asking better questions

(Source: McKinsey Global Institute)

## Why Expertise Alone Won't Save You

I've met brilliant technologists who got left behind. Not because they weren't smart — they were among the smartest people I've known. But they stopped being curious.

They became experts in one thing and defended that expertise like a fortress.

Here's what I've seen over time: **the leaders who thrive through technology shifts aren't the ones with the deepest expertise. They're the ones who stay curious enough to let their expertise evolve.**

At Appvance, we see this firsthand. AI can now generate software tests directly from business requirements. Which means the testing experts who only knew how to write manual test scripts? Their expertise became a ceiling, not a floor.

But the ones who got curious about AI-powered testing? They became more valuable than ever. Because they understood both the domain and the new tools.

That's the pattern. Every single wave.

## The Curiosity-Leadership Connection

So what does curiosity actually look like in a leadership context?

It's not about reading every AI newsletter. It's not about being the first to try every new tool. And it's definitely not about pretending you understand something when you don't.

**Curiosity in leadership looks like this:**

-> Asking "why does this process exist?" before automating it
-> Saying "I don't know — let me find out" instead of faking expertise
-> Exploring adjacent fields that might connect to your domain
-> Testing small experiments before making big strategy bets
-> Listening to the youngest person in the room — they often see the shift first

When I invented soundproof drywall, I didn't start as a building materials expert. I was curious about a problem — noise travels through walls — and I followed that curiosity into viscoelastic polymers and constrained layer damping. I didn't know those terms at the outset. But curiosity led me there, and the result was a product with billions of dollars installed worldwide.

The same principle applies to leading through AI.

## What Most Leaders Get Wrong

Most leaders approach AI adoption like a technology project. They hire consultants. They build roadmaps. They set up committees.

And then nothing happens.

You know why? Because they skipped the curiosity step. They went straight to "implement" without spending enough time in "understand."

**The leaders who actually succeed with AI do something different:**

-> They use AI tools themselves — not just approve budgets for others to use them
-> They ask naive questions on purpose — "Why can't we do this?" is more powerful than "Here's our Q3 AI roadmap"
-> They create space for experimentation — small, fast, low-stakes tests that teach the organization how to learn

I've talked about this in keynotes at some of the world's biggest conferences. And the reaction is always the same — people nod, because they've seen the committee approach fail.

The alternative isn't chaos. It's directed curiosity.

## The Joy Connection

Here's where it gets interesting.

In my book *The Joy-Success Cycle*, I make the case that curiosity is the engine of joy — and joy is the engine of sustained success. Not the other way around.

Most people think you work hard, succeed, and then feel good. But the research says otherwise. And my own experience — building companies, holding patents, navigating personal loss — confirms it.

**Joy comes from curiosity.** When you're genuinely curious, you're engaged. When you're engaged, you learn. When you learn, you grow. When you grow, you find purpose. And purpose creates joy.

That cycle — curiosity → learning → purpose → joy → success — is what separates leaders who burn out from leaders who sustain.

-> Pressure can drive results
-> But only joy sustains them

So when I tell leaders to get curious about AI, I'm not just giving career advice. I'm giving them a path to actually enjoying the ride instead of white-knuckling through it.

## What to Do This Week

I'm not going to give you a 47-step AI adoption framework. Here's what actually works:

**Pick one thing you do every week that feels repetitive.** Just one. Then spend 30 minutes exploring whether AI can handle part of it.

Don't try to transform your organization. Try to transform one hour of your week.

-> Use an AI tool to draft something you'd normally write from scratch
-> Ask an AI to analyze data you'd normally eyeball
-> Let an AI summarize a meeting you'd normally take 20 minutes to recap

That's it. That's the curiosity muscle. And like any muscle, it gets stronger the more you use it.

## The Leaders Who'll Win the Next Decade

I've lived through enough technology waves to recognize the pattern. The winners aren't the ones with the best strategy decks. They're the ones who stayed curious when everyone else was scared.

AI is the biggest shift since the internet. Maybe bigger. And the leaders who'll thrive aren't the ones who know the most about AI today.

**They're the ones who are most curious about what AI means tomorrow.**

Curiosity is what keeps you learning. Learning is what keeps you relevant. And relevance — real, earned, evolving relevance — is the only job security that actually works.

That's always been true. It just matters more now.
