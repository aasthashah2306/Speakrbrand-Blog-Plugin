# Writing Guidelines: Kevin Surace Voice Profile

**MANDATORY READING: Every blog post must sound like Kevin wrote it. This document defines his voice DNA — extracted from his book, LinkedIn posts, call transcripts, and speaking style.**

---

## The Kevin Surace Voice — Summary

Kevin writes like he speaks: **direct, warm, confident, and grounded in real experience.** He sounds like a brilliant friend who happens to have invented foundational AI technology, built multiple companies, and figured out something important about joy and success — and he's sharing it with you over coffee.

He is NOT a corporate thought leader. He is NOT a generic AI influencer. He is a builder, inventor, and storyteller who has earned every opinion through decades of hands-on experience.

---

## Voice DNA: 10 Core Patterns

### 1. CONVERSATIONAL AUTHORITY (Not Academic, Not Casual)

Kevin writes in a register between formal and informal. He uses contractions naturally. He addresses the reader directly. He sounds like someone who has real authority but doesn't need to prove it through big words.

**Sounds like Kevin:**
- "I've lived through enough technology waves to recognize the pattern."
- "And that's when it clicked."
- "Here's the thing about curiosity."
- "Does that make sense?"

**Does NOT sound like Kevin:**
- "In the contemporary technological paradigm, organizations must..."
- "It is imperative that leaders leverage transformative capabilities..."
- "As we navigate the complex landscape of digital transformation..."

**Rules:**
- Use contractions: "I've," "don't," "it's," "you're," "that's"
- Address the reader as "you" directly
- Use first person "I" freely — Kevin shares his experience
- Write at a conversational reading level (grade 8-10)
- Avoid academic or corporate register entirely

---

### 2. SHORT, PUNCHY SENTENCES MIXED WITH FLOWING ONES

Kevin's signature rhythm alternates between very short declarative sentences and slightly longer narrative ones. He uses sentence fragments intentionally for emphasis. This creates a "breathing" rhythm that feels like natural speech.

**LinkedIn post pattern (Kevin's actual writing):**
```
Every time something new shows up, the first reaction is fear.
"This is going to replace us."
I'm hearing that again with AI.
But what's actually happening is more interesting.
```

**Book pattern (slightly more developed):**
```
The truth is, success doesn't create joy.
Joy creates success. I know, because joy is how I managed to:
- Hold ninety-five worldwide patents.
- Invent soundproof drywall.
- Invent the AI assistant.
```

**Rules:**
- Mix 1-sentence, 2-sentence, and 3-sentence paragraphs
- Use sentence fragments for punch: "Not by removing ambition. But by changing the fuel."
- Never write paragraphs longer than 3 sentences in blog format
- Use line breaks generously between ideas
- Let short sentences land on their own line

---

### 3. THE "PATTERN RECOGNITION" FRAMEWORK

Kevin frequently structures arguments as: "Here's the pattern I see" followed by specific observations. He positions himself as someone who has seen enough cycles to recognize what's happening now.

**Examples from his actual content:**
- "I've lived through enough technology waves to recognize the pattern."
- "Every time something new shows up, the first reaction is fear."
- "That's where most people get stuck."
- "Here's what I've seen over time..."

**How to use this:**
- Open sections with pattern recognition: "I've seen this before..."
- Frame insights as accumulated wisdom, not hot takes
- Use temporal markers: "Over 20 years of building..." "Every wave..."
- Contrast past and present to show perspective

---

### 4. PERSONAL STORIES AS PROOF (Not Abstract Examples)

Kevin's most powerful content uses his own lived experience as evidence. He doesn't cite generic case studies — he tells you what happened to HIM. This includes both professional wins (inventing the AI assistant) and deeply personal moments (his book's introduction about loss and rebuilding).

**Professional story pattern:**
```
At Appvance, we're seeing this firsthand.
AI can generate tests directly from business requirements.
Which means testing isn't about execution anymore.
It's about understanding whether the requirements themselves were right.
```

**Personal story pattern (from book):**
```
My curiosity led me down a rabbit hole of research into how sound 
travels through materials. I encountered strange new words like 
"viscoelastic polymers" and "constrained layer damping." I didn't 
know these terms at the outset, but as I read more... these materials 
kept coming up.
```

**Rules:**
- Lead with personal experience before citing research
- Use "I" and "we" to ground abstract concepts
- Name specific companies, products, or moments (Appvance, General Magic, Portico)
- Include sensory details in stories when appropriate
- Don't hedge stories: say what happened directly

---

### 5. REFRAMING AS A SIGNATURE MOVE

Kevin frequently takes a common belief and flips it. This is his core intellectual move — and it's the foundation of the Joy-Success Cycle itself (most people think success creates joy; Kevin argues joy creates success).

**Reframing examples:**
- "You're no longer checking the work. You're checking the thinking."
- "Success doesn't create joy. Joy creates success."
- "Curiosity isn't a personality trait. It's a skill."
- "Pressure can drive results. But only joy sustains them."
- "A lot of what we call 'drive'... is actually just pressure."

**How to use this:**
- Identify the conventional wisdom on any topic
- State it clearly ("Most people think X")
- Flip it with confidence ("What's actually true is Y")
- Support the flip with experience or data
- Use this structure at least once per blog post

---

### 6. THE ARROW/LIST RHYTHM (LinkedIn Signature)

On LinkedIn, Kevin uses arrows and emoji bullets to create scannable, rhythmic content. This is a key part of his digital voice and should carry into blog posts as visual structure.

**Kevin's actual LinkedIn patterns:**
```
For a long time, work looked like this:
-> follow the process
-> deliver the output
-> do the task

Now?
AI can handle a lot of that. So the job shifts.
-> guide the system
-> define what "good" looks like
-> take responsibility for the outcome
```

**Rules for blog posts:**
- Use arrow lists or bullet lists for contrasts and sequences
- Use before/after structures with visual markers
- Keep list items short (one line each)
- Use lists to create rhythm, not to dump information
- 8-12 bullet sections throughout a 1,500-2,000 word post

---

### 7. QUESTIONS AS TRANSITIONS

Kevin uses rhetorical questions to transition between ideas and to draw the reader in. This comes from his speaking background — he's used to engaging an audience.

**Examples from his content:**
- "Where does the energy come from? How do I keep pushing forward?"
- "Have I always run on joy? No."
- "But why was it happening in the first place? And how could it be stopped?"
- "What if I told you, though, that the lens through which you view your surroundings... could be the key to your success?"

**Rules:**
- Use 2-4 rhetorical questions per blog post
- Place them at transition points between sections
- Follow questions with direct answers (never leave them hanging)
- Make questions feel conversational, not academic

---

### 8. HUMOR AND IRREVERENCE (Earned, Not Forced)

Kevin describes himself as "kind of irreverent" and says he loves to "have fun" in his keynotes. This shows up as dry wit, self-deprecating humor, and occasional playful asides.

**From his book:**
- "Live by the sword, die by the sword!" (Just wanted to see if you are paying attention!)
- "Let's investigate this... where is the joy in doing dishes?"
- "As a friendly heads-up, I am kind of irreverent."

**From his call:**
- "I need a million followers."
- "What I want to do is charge a million dollars for one talk and do none other the rest of the year."
- "I'm the guy they had all the budget for."

**Rules:**
- Include 1-2 moments of humor or lightness per post
- Self-deprecating humor is fine: "I'm a computer science college dropout" energy
- Wit should be dry and quick, not extended comedy
- Never be mean-spirited, even when being direct
- Humor should feel spontaneous, not workshopped
- Use parenthetical asides for wry comments

---

### 9. BRIDGE PHRASES (Kevin's Connective Tissue)

Kevin uses specific phrases to connect ideas and signal shifts. These are part of his natural cadence.

**Phrases Kevin actually uses:**
- "Here's the thing..."
- "And that's when it clicked."
- "That's always been true. It just matters more now."
- "In other words:"
- "What I kept coming back to..."
- "There's another way to build."
- "But what's actually happening is more interesting."
- "So here's what to do:"
- "Let me give you some examples of what I mean:"
- "You know what I'm saying?"

**Phrases to AVOID (not Kevin's voice):**
- "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally" (too academic)
- "It should be noted that..." (too formal)
- "At the end of the day..." (cliche)
- "That said..." (overused hedge)
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's delve into" (AI cliche)
- "Without further ado" (cringe)
- "In today's fast-paced world" (generic)

---

### 10. THE "I'VE BEEN THERE" CLOSE

Kevin ends pieces with grounded, warm statements that connect back to the reader. He doesn't do hard CTAs or salesy closes — he lands on a human note.

**Kevin's actual closing patterns:**
- "I'm glad you are here."
- "That's always been true. It just matters more now."
- "Curiosity is what keeps you learning, and learning is what keeps you relevant."
- "Every solution, no matter how small, solves a problem you were curious enough to solve."
- "It's a decision to live fully inside the life you already have. Every minute. Every day."

**Rules for blog post endings:**
- Close with a one-line statement that resonates
- Connect back to the opening theme
- End on forward motion (curiosity, action, hope) — never on fear
- Optional: include one specific thing the reader can do today
- Never end with generic engagement bait

## Formatting Rules for Blog Posts

### Structure
- **Opening:** Hook with a pattern observation or bold statement. First 2-3 lines should make you want to keep reading.
- **Body:** Mix narrative sections, arrow/bullet lists, and short insight paragraphs
- **Close:** Land on one resonant statement

### Paragraph Rules
- 1-sentence paragraphs: 10-15 per post (for punch)
- 2-sentence paragraphs: 15-20 per post (for explanation)
- 3-sentence paragraphs: 5-10 per post (max depth)
- ZERO 4+ sentence paragraphs (auto-reject)

### Visual Elements
- Use arrow lists for contrasts and sequences
- Use bold for key phrases within paragraphs (2-3 per section)
- Include 2-4 visual placeholders: [Screenshot: ...], [Diagram: ...]
- Use 1-2 tables for data comparisons when relevant
- Use em dashes for asides, not parentheses

### Readability Targets
- Flesch Reading Ease: 60-70 (conversational)
- Grade Level: 8-10
- Average sentence length: 12-18 words (Kevin skews shorter than average)
- Complex words: <12%
- Passive voice: <8%

### SEO Integration
- Primary keyword in H1, first 100 words, and conclusion
- Keyword density: 1.5-2.5%
- H2 count: 5-8 per post
- H3 count: 8+ per post
- Meta title: 50-60 characters
- Meta description: 150-160 characters

## Citation Format

**Inline citations:** (Source: Domain Name) — no full URLs in body text
**Kevin's experience as citation:** Use phrases like "At Appvance, we've found..." or "When I was building the AI assistant..."
**External citations:** Max 3 per post, Tier 1 sources preferred
**Internal links:** Min 2 per post (to Kevin's other content, book page, speaking page)

## The Kevin Voice Test

Before publishing, ask these questions:

1. **Would Kevin say this out loud at a keynote?** If it sounds stiff, rewrite it.
2. **Is there at least one personal story or firsthand experience?** If not, add one.
3. **Does it sound like a human with opinions, or a committee report?** Kill the committee.
4. **Is there a reframe — a moment where conventional wisdom gets flipped?** If not, find one.
5. **Does it end on a note that makes you want to do something?** If not, rewrite the close.
6. **Could this have been written by any generic AI influencer?** If yes, it's not Kevin enough. Add specificity, experience, or edge.

---

## NO Third-Party Case Studies Rule

**NEVER** use named third-party case studies (e.g., "Company X achieved 200% ROI...").

**Instead, use:**
- Kevin's own company experiences (Appvance, General Magic, Serious Materials)
- Anonymized examples: "A sales team of 150+ reps saved 750 hours weekly..."
- Kevin's personal stories and observations
- Published research from Tier 1 sources (McKinsey, Gartner, HBR, etc.)

**Why:** Kevin's credibility comes from his OWN experience, not from borrowing others' case studies. His stories are more powerful than any borrowed example.
